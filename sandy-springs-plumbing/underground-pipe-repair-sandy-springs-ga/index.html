    Frequently Asked Questions About Underground Pipe Repair in Sandy Springs

    A single repair on a buried water line typically costs $800 to $2,500 including locating and excavation. Full main water service replacement typically runs $2,500 to $6,500, and sewer lateral repairs range from $1,500 to $5,000 depending on how deep the pipe is.

    Ignoring an underground pipe leak can lead to significant problems, including costly water damage to your foundation, landscaping erosion, mold growth, higher water bills and potential contamination of your water supply. It's important to address these issues promptly.

    Small repairs often do not need a permit, but full water service or sewer line replacement usually does. Work in the public right-of-way always requires coordination with the city. We handle permits when required.

    For water lines we typically use PEX or Type K copper, both approved for direct burial. For sewer lines we use Schedule 40 PVC. The right choice depends on the existing pipe and local code.

    Trenchless pipe repair offers several key advantages, including minimal disruption to your yard, landscaping and concrete, which can save on restoration costs. It's usually faster than traditional excavation and less invasive, preserving the look and value of your property. This method is also durable, often extending the lifespan of your pipes.

    The time of an underground pipe repair depends on the complexity. A minor leak repair might take a few hours, while a full trenchless sewer line replacement could take one to three days. We always aim for prompt service with minimal disruption.

    HGR Plumbing uses high-quality, industry-approved materials from trusted brands known for durability and longevity. This includes PEX piping from manufacturers like Uponor or Viega, and various PVC and ABS pipe products that meet ASTM standards.

    We use acoustic listening devices that pick up the sound of escaping water, tracer gas that rises through soil at the leak point and electronic locators that trace the pipe path. Together these narrow the leak to a small area.
