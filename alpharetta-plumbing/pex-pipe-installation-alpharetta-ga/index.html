    Manufacturer rules for PEX are specific: horizontal supports roughly every 32 inches, a minimum bend radius, and no long sun exposure. Our crews follow them. Ignoring them causes kinks, sags and strained fittings, and it can cost you the manufacturer warranty that should have backed the pipe.

    • Chlorine Rated, Code-listed PEX is tested for chlorinated city water at hot-water temperatures.

    What Goes Into a PEX Install in Alpharetta, GA

    PEX-B Crimp and Clamp Systems in Alpharetta

    PEX-B is stiffer, costs less per foot, and joins with copper crimp rings or stainless cinch clamps over brass or poly-alloy fittings. A crimp joint is only as good as the tool, so we check every ring with a go/no-go gauge, and a ring that does not pass gets cut out and redone. It is a five-second check. Skipping it causes most crimp leaks.

    PEX for Remodels & Additions in Alpharetta, GA

    Opening a wall for a kitchen remodel is the cheapest time to replace old supply lines. With the studs bare, we can run PEX back to a manifold or to the nearest good trunk, set new stub-outs for the sink and dishwasher, and add a line for the fridge ice maker. An addition gets sized on its own. A far-end bathroom may need a three-quarter inch feed to hold pressure.

    Hydronic Heating with PEX in Alpharetta

    Radiant floor loops are PEX too, though not the same pipe you drink from. Heating work calls for oxygen-barrier PEX, coated so oxygen cannot seep through the pipe wall and corrode the boiler, the circulator and the iron parts. Loops have length limits. Run them too long and the last stretch never warms up.

    PEX-A Expansion Fitting Systems in Alpharetta, GA

    With PEX-A, the tech slips an expansion ring onto the pipe and stretches both with a battery tool. The fitting is pushed in while the pipe is open, and the plastic's memory shrinks it back around the barb, sealing tighter during the first minute. In the cold, it takes longer to shrink back. We let joints set before testing.

    PEX-A, PEX-B, and PEX-C Systems in Alpharetta, GA

    There are three grades of PEX, named for how the plastic is cross-linked at the factory. PEX-A is the most flexible and joins with expansion rings, PEX-B is stiffer and joins with crimp rings or cinch clamps, and PEX-C sits closer to B in feel. Every one is listed for potable water. Pipe, fittings and tools come from the same maker on our jobs, which keeps the warranty valid.

    Uponor PEX System Installation in Alpharetta

    Uponor's ProPEX system is PEX-A joined with an expansion tool plus a ring slipped over the pipe end. The tech expands the pipe and ring together, pushes the fitting in, and the plastic shrinks back around the barb over the next few seconds, tighter than when it started. No crimp tool to calibrate. The fittings have a wide bore, so flow loss through each joint stays low.

    PEX Repipe Cost Comparison in Alpharetta, GA

    Price on a repipe comes down to a few things: bathroom count, square footage, how many stories, and how easy the attic and walls are to work in. PEX usually beats copper on price because the pipe costs less and a crew finishes in two to four days, not a full week. Drywall patching may be separate. We price PEX-A, PEX-B and copper side by side when you ask.

    PEX-A vs. PEX-B Installation in Alpharetta

    Homeowners ask us which one is better, and the honest answer depends on the job. PEX-A bends tighter, recovers from a kink with a heat gun, and its expansion fittings flow more water, while PEX-B costs less and its crimp joints are just as reliable when they are gauged properly. Either one lasts for decades. For tight attics we lean toward PEX-A.
