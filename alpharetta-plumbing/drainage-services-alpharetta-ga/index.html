    Jetting for Scale and Grease in Alpharetta

    Jetting uses water at high pressure to strip a pipe back to its original diameter.     Storm Drain and French Drain Installation in Alpharetta, GA

    Heavy downpours expose every low spot in a yard.     French Drain Installation in Alpharetta

    A French drain is a gravel-filled trench with perforated pipe that collects groundwater and carries it away. Filter fabric around the stone keeps silt out. The pipe needs at least a 1 percent slope to a daylight outlet, a dry well or a sump, and the outlet end needs a grate so animals do not nest in it.

    Channel and Trench Drains in Alpharetta, GA

    A typical French drain or downspout system takes one to three days. Bigger jobs take longer. Projects with dry wells, long trench runs, or concrete that has to be saw-cut for a channel drain can take most of a week, especially if the ground is too wet to dig cleanly the first day.

    Roots grow toward the water and nutrients inside sewer pipe. They only need a hairline gap. Once a root tip gets in through a cracked joint or loose seal, it grows into a mass that catches paper and grease, and clay and older concrete pipe with many joints are the most vulnerable.

    Drain cleaning usually means a cable machine with a cutting head that bores through the clog. It is quick and cheap. Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to scrub the whole pipe wall clean of grease, scale and roots, so it lasts longer but costs more and needs a pipe that is in good enough shape to take the pressure.

    They solve different problems. A French drain collects water that soaks into the soil. A channel drain catches surface water sheeting across concrete, like a driveway that slopes toward the garage, and many yards need one of each because the water arrives both ways during a hard storm.

    Snaking is the right first step for most single clogs. It is fast and inexpensive. Jetting wins for grease, scale and recurring roots since it cleans the whole wall.
