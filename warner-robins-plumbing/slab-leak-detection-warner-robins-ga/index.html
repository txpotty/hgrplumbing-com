    Frequently Asked Questions About Slab Leak Detection in Warner Robins

    Slab leak detection in Warner Robins usually costs $150 to $450. Repairs range from about $800 for a simple reroute to $3,000 or more for direct access under finished floors. We provide a written estimate after locating the leak.

    A water line slab leak involves pressurized fresh water escaping from supply pipes, often resulting in higher water bills, warm spots or the sound of running water. A sewer line slab leak involves wastewater leaking from drain pipes, which usually causes foul odors, slow drains or visible sewage seepage, and is not usually pressurized.

    While the detection process itself usually doesn't require a permit, any subsequent repair work involving the excavation of the slab or significant plumbing alterations will likely require a plumbing permit from the local building department. HGR Plumbing handles all necessary permits for repair work.

    Once the slab leak is precisely detected, our technician will provide you with a detailed report and discuss the best repair options. These options may include trenchless repair, re-routing the affected pipe or traditional slab excavation and repair. We will then provide a transparent estimate for the repair work.

    Reroutes and repipes often require a plumbing permit, while small direct repairs may not. Requirements vary by city and county. We take care of permits and inspections when needed.

    Common signs of a slab leak include surprisingly high water bills, warm spots on your floor, the sound of running water when no fixtures are in use, visible cracks in the foundation, damp or wet spots on carpets or flooring and a sudden drop in water pressure. Musty odors can also indicate moisture buildup.

    Direct repair works well for a single leak on otherwise healthy pipe in an accessible spot. Rerouting is often better under tile or hardwood and avoids future leaks on that line. We help you compare both.

    While you can look for the signs, precisely detecting a slab leak requires specialized equipment and expertise that most homeowners don't have. DIY attempts often lead to more damage or misdiagnosis, making professional detection a more cost-effective and reliable solution in the long run.
