    Frequently Asked Questions About Rooter Services in Columbus

    The cost of rooter service in Columbus, GA usually ranges from $200 to $600, depending on the severity and location of the clog. More complex issues like main sewer line blockages or those requiring hydro-jetting can be higher, generally between $400 and $1,000. We provide transparent, upfront pricing after a thorough diagnosis.

    Most standard rooter services for localized clogs can be completed within 1 to 3 hours. More complex issues, such as main sewer line root removal or extensive hydro-jetting, may take longer, potentially 3 to 6 hours. We aim to complete the job promptly while staying thorough.

    Generally, simple rooter services to clear clogs do not require a permit in Columbus. However, if the service reveals a need for pipe repair or replacement, especially for main sewer lines, a plumbing permit may be required. We will handle all required permitting if that situation arises.

    Homes with tree roots or older clay pipe often need rooting every 12 to 24 months. Homes with newer PVC lines may go years without it. A camera inspection can help set the right schedule.

    While chemical drain cleaners are available, HGR Plumbing generally advises against them. They often only partially clear clogs, can damage pipes over time, and pose safety risks. Professional rooter services are much more effective, safer for your plumbing, and provide a lasting fix to blockages.

    Drain snaking, also known as augering, uses a long, flexible cable with a cutting head to physically break through or retrieve blockages like hair or tree roots. Hydro-jetting uses high-pressure water to clean the entire interior of the pipe, removing grease, mineral buildup, and sludge, leaving pipes much cleaner than snaking alone. Hydro-jetting is generally more effective for severe or recurring clogs.

    Slow drains in several fixtures, gurgling toilets and sewage odors are common signs. Water backing up into a tub when you flush is another clue. Frequent clogs in the same line also suggest a deeper blockage.

    Rooting uses a spinning cable with cutting blades to break up clogs and roots. Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to scrub the pipe walls. Many tough lines do best with both.
