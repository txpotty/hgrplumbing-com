    Gas Meter to House Line Repair in Columbus, GA

    The run from the utility meter to the house shutoff is the homeowner's pipe in most service areas, so the repair falls to a plumber and not the gas company. That stretch sees more weather than any other pipe in the system. Risers rust at grade. We replace the riser or the buried run, pressure test it and leave the meter connection ready for the utility to open the meter. Expect a half day for a typical riser swap.

    Underground Gas Line Repair in Columbus

    Yard lines run from the meter to the house, and often on to a detached garage, a pool heater, a grill or an outdoor kitchen. Steel pipe in the ground corrodes wherever the wrap was nicked during the original install. Roots and settling do damage too. After an 811 locate, we excavate, cut out the failed run and lay polyethylene with a tracer wire beside it. Sand goes around the new pipe before backfill, per Columbus trench rules.

    Gas Leak Repair Cost Comparison in Columbus, GA

    Price follows access. Expect $150 to $450 for a connector or fitting repair you can reach, with the leak test included. Interior pipe inside finished walls or attics often lands between $300 and $1,000, and a buried yard line can run $1,000 to $4,000 depending on length, depth and whether we can bore instead of trench. The written estimate comes after the leak is located and before any repair begins. Permit fees are listed as their own line.

    Propane Gas Leak Repair in Columbus, GA

    Propane behaves differently from natural gas because it is heavier than air, so a leak settles into basements and crawlspaces instead of rising. The first-stage regulator at the tank and the second-stage regulator at the house both get checked. Low spots get sniffed first. If the supplier owns the tank, they handle the tank side and we handle the piping from there to the appliances. Rural properties with underground tanks get a line test too.

    Gas Line Repair & Replacement in Columbus, GA

    Black iron pipe with threaded fittings is still the most common interior gas pipe, and the leak is nearly always at a thread, not in the middle of a length. The joint gets broken, cleaned and remade with new pipe dope rated for gas. Cracked fittings get replaced. Badly rusted sections, often near a crawlspace vent, are cut back to clean pipe and replaced. Every remade joint gets a detector check and a gauge test.

    Permitting and Code Compliance in Columbus, GA

    Most jurisdictions want a permit for new gas pipe or a moved line, though a straight connector swap often does not need one. You will know before we start whether a permit applies. The fee goes on the estimate. The work follows Georgia adopted fuel gas code and any local amendments. The signed inspection card goes to you, and a future buyer's inspector will likely ask to see it.

    Residential Gas Leak Detection in Columbus

    A gas leak in a house rarely sits where the smell is strongest, because gas drifts along joists and collects in closets and basements. We start with a gauge on the system to confirm there is a leak at all. Then the sniffer comes out. A combustible gas detector follows the reading from fitting to fitting until it spikes. Bubble solution on the suspect joint gives the final confirmation.

    Red Tag Gas Repairs in Columbus

    A red tag on your meter or appliance means the utility found a leak or unsafe condition and will not restore gas until a licensed contractor fixes it. The tag usually says what they found, so we start there. Then we test everything. After the repair, we run the pressure test the utility or inspector requires and send them the paperwork. Relights are scheduled with the utility, usually within a day.
