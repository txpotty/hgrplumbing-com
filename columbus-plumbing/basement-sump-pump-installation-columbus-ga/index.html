    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Columbus

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit usually runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. Cutting a new pit through the slab typically costs $1,500 to $3,500, and a battery backup adds roughly $600 to $1,500. Most people add the backup.

    Look for a pump that runs nonstop, short cycles, or does not start when the pit fills. Visible rust and a stuck float are other red flags, and if your basement flooded even though you have a pump, the pump or its switch was not up to the job. Over seven years old is suspect.

    A 1/3 or 1/2 horsepower pump handles most residential basements. What decides it is how fast the pit fills and how high the water rises, so we time the refill with a stopwatch and measure from the pit floor to the discharge exit before picking a model. Oversizing causes short cycling.

    Zoeller, Wayne, Little Giant and Liberty Pumps cover most of our installs. Horsepower and lift decide which model goes in, then by features like a vertical float or a built-in alarm, so the pump fits your pit, your budget and the amount of water your Columbus basement actually takes on. Cast iron is our default.

    Grinding or rattling during a cycle is the first warning. Other signs are rust on the housing and rapid cycling, along with a pump that does not start during a heavy rain even though the pit is full to the float. Past ten years, replace it.

    Swapping an identical plug-in pump is doable for a handy homeowner. New pits, new outlets and discharge routing are a different story, because a mistake there leads to a cracked slab, a shock hazard, or water pumped right back against the foundation in Columbus.
