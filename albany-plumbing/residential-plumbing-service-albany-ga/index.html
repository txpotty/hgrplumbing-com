    Frequently Asked Questions About Residential Plumbing Service in Albany

    Pricing depends on the job. Simple repairs like a faucet cartridge or toilet rebuild tend to cost $150 to $350, a water heater installation is often $1,200 to $3,500, and slab leak repairs or full repipes can reach several thousand dollars. The exact number comes after the diagnosis.

    Once a year is a good rule. Hard water speeds up sediment buildup, so homes with scale may need a flush more often, and a yearly check lets us test the relief valve, look at the anode rod and catch a weeping fitting before it rusts through the jacket. An anode rod often wears out in three to five years.

    Every repair comes with a written parts and labor warranty. New fixtures and water heaters also carry manufacturer coverage, and we register it for you, which matters because some water heater makers shorten the tank warranty if the unit is never registered. Save the serial number.

    Hard water carries dissolved calcium and magnesium. A softener swaps those minerals out through ion exchange, and the difference shows up as scale in water heaters, white crust on faucets, spotty dishes and soap that will not lather well. Fixtures last longer on soft water.

    Two leaks in a year is the tipping point for most homes. Polybutylene or galvanized supply pipe pushes it toward a repipe, too, since patching one spot on a failing system just moves the next leak a few feet down the line. We price both side by side.

    A slab leak is a break in a water line running under a concrete foundation. Much of Georgia sits on heavy red clay that swells when wet and shrinks in drought, which can stress slab plumbing and sewer laterals over time. The usual signs are a warm patch of floor, a water bill that jumps or water running when nothing is on. Do not wait on it.
