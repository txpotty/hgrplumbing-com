    Frequently Asked Questions About Drainage Services in Albany

    Cost depends on whether it is a clog or a construction job. A drain clearing usually stays under a few hundred dollars. Jetting and sewer repairs cost more, and yard drains are priced by length and digging. You get a written quote first.

    Most sink and toilet clogs clear in under an hour. A main line needs more time. A main line with camera work usually takes about two hours, more if the cleanout is hard to reach.

    Snaking is the right first step for most single clogs. It costs less and takes less time. Jetting wins for grease, scale and recurring roots since it cleans the whole wall.

    A single fixture drain usually takes about an hour. Main lines take one to two hours. Camera work adds 30 to 60 minutes, and a buried or missing cleanout adds more.

    Store-bought cleaners rarely clear a real clog. They can make things worse. The chemicals sit in the trap, heat up and can damage older pipe or soften glue joints, and if the clog does not clear, the plumber now has to open a trap full of caustic liquid. A cable is safer.

    Simple downspout extensions are a reasonable DIY project. French drains are harder. They need proper slope and a legal outlet, and digging without an 811 locate is risky.

    Slow sinks and gurgling drains are the early signs. A sewer smell in the yard or basement comes next. Outside, look for water that stands more than a day after rain, soggy spots along the foundation, or mulch and soil washed onto the walk, all of which mean water is not leaving the yard the way it should.

    Watch for water pooling near the house after storms. Washed-out mulch tells you too. Inside, a damp crawlspace, a musty basement or efflorescence on the foundation wall all mean water is reaching the house instead of being carried away, and the problem usually starts at a downspout or a low spot.
