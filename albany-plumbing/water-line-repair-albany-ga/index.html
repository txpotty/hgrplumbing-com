    What Our Water Line Repair Covers in Albany, GA

    Main Water Line Replacement in Albany, GA

    Replacement beats repair once a line has leaked two or three times, or when it is galvanized or polybutylene. Patching that kind of pipe tends to buy months, not years. We run new Type K copper or PEX from the meter to the house, typically finishing in one day whether we trench or pull the pipe. We pull the permit, set up the inspection and pressure test the new line before it is covered.

    Main Water Line Leak Repair in Albany

    A meter that moves with every fixture off is the clearest sign you have. We confirm it in about ten minutes. The house shutoff gets closed, the dial gets watched, and if it keeps creeping we know the leak is between the meter and the house. Ground microphones find the spot. A repair coupling or a new length of pipe goes in, and then we pressure test it.

    Galvanized Pipe Water Line Repair in Albany, GA

    Galvanized steel service lines rust from the inside out. By the time one leaks, the rest of the pipe is close behind. We can clamp or replace a short section to get you through, and sometimes that buys a few good years. More often we recommend replacing the whole run from the meter to the house with copper or PEX. You get your flow back as a bonus.

    PEX and Copper Water Line Repair in Albany

    Copper and PEX fail in different places. Copper tends to pinhole where a rock rubs it or where the water chemistry eats the wall from inside. With PEX, the fitting is the weak point most of the time. We cut out the bad section of copper and sweat or press in a new length, and for PEX we use the matching crimp or expansion tool so the joint is made the way the manufacturer intends.

    Pressure Regulator and Shutoff Repair in Albany

    High static pressure shortens the life of the service line and every fixture behind it. Most codes cap it at 80 psi. We gauge the pressure at a hose bib, and if it reads high we replace the pressure reducing valve with a Watts or Zurn Wilkins unit. Old gate valves that will not close get replaced with full-port ball valves. That way you can actually shut the water off.

    Galvanized Water Line Replacement in Albany, GA

    Galvanized service lines were common in houses built before the 1970s, and a lot of them are still in the ground. Private wells still serve a large share of rural Georgia homes, and iron, sediment, and low pH can shorten the life of pumps, tanks, and copper lines. Rust and scale close the pipe from the inside. Rusty water at the first tap in the morning is a classic sign, and so is pressure that falls off when the shower and the washer run together. Replacing the full run with copper or PEX clears all of it up. Usually a one-day job.

    Copper Water Line Repair in Albany

    Buried copper is tough, but it is not forever. Rocks pressing against the pipe wear pinholes over time, and acidic soil or high water velocity can thin the wall from the inside. We usually cut out two feet. The new piece goes in with soldered or pressed couplings, then we look over the pipe on either side for more pitting before we backfill.

    Slab Leak Water Line Repair in Albany

    A slab leak is a supply pipe leaking under the concrete floor of the house. Older in-town homes often have galvanized steel supply lines that corrode from the inside, while newer construction mostly uses PEX or CPVC. Signs include a warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water and a meter that moves with everything off. Acoustic gear and a thermal camera tell us where it is. From there you have three choices: break the slab at the spot, reroute the line through the walls or attic, or abandon the old pipe under the floor and run new PEX above it. We price each one for you.
