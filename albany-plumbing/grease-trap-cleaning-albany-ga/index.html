    Grease Line Jetting in Albany

    Grease that slips past a full trap coats the 4-inch line out to the main. The buildup forms rings that are thickest along the bottom. A snake cuts a narrow path through and the line plugs again within weeks, while a jetter with a rotating nozzle peels the grease off the whole pipe wall. We run a camera after to confirm.

    Grease Trap Cleaning Cost in Albany, GA

    Cost follows trap size and fill level. Small under-sink traps go quickly. A 1,500-gallon interceptor that has gone five months without pumping needs a vacuum truck, a pressure wand and extra disposal fees for the volume, so it lands at the high end. A steady schedule lowers what each visit costs.

    Hydro-Jetting for Grease Lines in Albany, GA

    A cable machine pokes a hole through grease. A jetter takes it off the pipe wall. We run a jetter at roughly 3,000 to 4,000 psi with a rotating nozzle that cuts the grease off the full circumference of the pipe and flushes it downstream. A camera pass afterward shows whether the line is really clean.

    Emergency Grease Trap Services in Albany, GA

    Gray water coming up through the floor sinks in the middle of service is the classic sign the trap has hit capacity. Same-day trucks are the goal on emergency calls. First the trap gets pumped, and then we check the line past the outlet, since a lateral packed with grease will not clear just from pumping. Sometimes it needs jetting too.

    Grease Trap Cleaning for Restaurants in Albany, GA

    Menu matters more than seat count. A sushi bar and a fried chicken place of the same size can need pumping on two completely different schedules. Our questions are about the line, the fryer count and how often the dish machine cycles. Then we measure.

    Preventive Maintenance Programs in Albany, GA

    A maintenance plan is just a pumping interval plus someone who tracks it. We keep the calendar so your manager does not have to.     Under-Sink Grease Trap Maintenance in Albany, GA

    Under-sink hydromechanical traps are rated by flow, usually 10 to 50 gallons per minute, and they hold far less than people expect. In a busy kitchen, that means cleaning every week or two. We take the lid off, remove grease and solids, scrape the baffles, check the flow control fitting on the inlet, and replace the lid gasket if it is flattened or torn. A bad gasket is why most of these smell.
