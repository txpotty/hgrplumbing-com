    How We Install Kitchen Sinks in Albany, GA: Style by Style

    Restaurant Sink Installation in Albany

    Restaurants, cafes and commissary kitchens in Albany get their commercial sinks installed by HGR Plumbing. Hand sinks need tempered or hot and cold water. Three-compartment, prep and hand sinks go in with the floor sinks, indirect drains and grease trap tie-ins the inspector checks.

    Farmhouse Sink Plumbing in Albany, GA

    Fireclay and cast iron farmhouse sinks can weigh well over 100 pounds empty, before a single drop of water goes in. We build a support frame inside the cabinet and set the sink level before the counter is installed. Deep bowls need a lower trap. The drain gets planned for that depth from day one.

    Top-mount Sink Installation in Albany

    Drop-in sinks are common in Georgia homes with laminate or tile counters. The rim holds the sink up. After checking the cutout, we lay silicone under the lip and draw the clips down evenly so the whole rim sits flat. Then the drain and faucet get connected and tested.

    Composite Sink Installation in Albany

    Granite and quartz composite sinks resist scratches on the surface but can chip at the drain hole if a strainer is overtightened. Hand tight comes first. Most composite brands want silicone rather than putty under the strainer because putty can stain them, so we go by the maker's instructions.

    Apron-Front Sinks in Albany, GA

    Apron sinks need a cabinet cut to fit the front panel, and the bowl must be set before the counter gets measured. We set the sink and frame early, so the fabricator measures from the actual bowl instead of a spec sheet. It prevents a crooked reveal. Cast iron versions can top 150 pounds, so we bring two people.

    Undermount Kitchen Sink Installation in Albany

    Undermount sinks rely on clips and adhesive to stay attached to granite or quartz. We mount the bowl, seal the rim with silicone and brace it while the adhesive sets, which usually takes overnight before the sink can hold a full load of water. Then come the strainers and trap.

    Drop-In Sink Replacement in Albany

    Removing an old drop-in begins by slicing through the old caulk and backing off the clips from below. Old clips are often rusted solid, so we bring a reciprocating saw and cut them off rather than prying on the counter. Laminate chips if you pry. The new sink needs to cover the old cutout fully.

    New Construction Sink Rough-In in Albany, GA

    Rough-in sets the drain height and the stops long before the cabinets arrive. We usually set the kitchen drain stub around 16 to 20 inches off the floor, then adjust for deep bowls or a disposal so the trap does not end up below the wall outlet. Venting happens at this stage. Having the sink and faucet specs early saves moving pipe later.
