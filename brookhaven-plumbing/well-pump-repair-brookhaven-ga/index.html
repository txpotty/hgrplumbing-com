    Pressure Switch Replacement in Brookhaven

    The pressure switch tells the pump when to start and stop. Most switches fail from pitted contacts, a plugged sensing tube or insects in the housing. We replace the switch, clean or replace the tube and set the cut-in and cut-out pressures to match your tank, typically 40 and 60 psi. This is among the least expensive well repairs.

    Grundfos Well Pump Repair in Brookhaven

    Grundfos SQ and SQE pumps have built-in electronics that protect the motor and report faults. Most Grundfos calls involve a sensor or a drive fault. HGR Plumbing reads the fault code, checks the wiring and tests the pressure sensor before replacing anything in a Brookhaven, GA well. Electronics fail less often than people think.

    Deep Well Pump Repair in Brookhaven

    Deep wells get submersible pumps. They push water up instead of pulling it, and they run quiet for years, but motors, impellers and wire splices still fail eventually. We pull the pump with the right equipment, test it and replace only what failed. Heat-shrink splice kits and a torque arrestor protect the new work in your Brookhaven, GA well.

    Well Pump Brands We Service in Brookhaven, GA

    Goulds, Grundfos, Franklin Electric and Myers make most of the pumps we pull in this area, and HGR Plumbing services all of them. Older units and newer variable speed systems are both familiar.     Constant Pressure System Repair in Brookhaven, GA

    Variable speed drives like the Grundfos SQE and Franklin SubDrive keep pressure steady by changing motor speed. When they fault, the drive shows a code that guides diagnosis. The drive, sensor and wiring get tested, and the failed part gets repaired or replaced before we verify the setpoint. These need a smaller tank.

    Jet Pump Repair in Brookhaven, GA

    Pressure Tank Problems in Brookhaven

    Short cycling is the classic pressure tank symptom, with the pump clicking on and off every few seconds while a faucet runs. A waterlogged tank or a ruptured bladder causes most of it. We check the precharge with the tank drained. It should sit about 2 psi below the cut-in pressure, so a 40/60 switch wants roughly 38 psi in the tank.
