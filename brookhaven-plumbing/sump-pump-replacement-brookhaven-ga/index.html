    Float Switch and Check Valve Replacement in Brookhaven

    Many sump pump failures start with a stuck float switch or a leaking check valve. A failed check valve lets water flow back into the pit and causes the pump to short cycle. We install new check valves with every pump replacement and verify the switch moves freely. Vertical float switches work well in tight pits. Tethered floats suit larger pits.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Sump Pump Replacement in Brookhaven

    A standard sump pump replacement typically costs $500 to $1,200 installed. Battery backup systems usually add $800 to $1,800. Sewage ejector pumps generally cost $1,200 to $2,500.

    Most sump pump replacements take one to two hours. Battery backups or new discharge lines take a bit longer.

    If storms knock out power in your area or you have finished living space below grade, a backup is a smart investment. A battery unit with a high-water alarm typically runs six to ten hours of intermittent pumping on a full charge.

    In many areas, minor like-for-like sump pump replacements typically do not require a permit. However, if the replacement involves significant changes to the electrical system, discharge piping or the sump pit itself, a permit might be necessary. Our team will advise you on local code requirements in Brookhaven.

    While it might seem like a simple task, sump pump replacement involves electrical connections, proper plumbing and making sure the pump is correctly sized and installed to prevent future flooding. DIY replacement can lead to improper operation, electrical hazards or system failure. It is best to hire a professional plumber for a safe and effective installation.

    A battery backup sump pump system is a secondary pump powered by a battery, designed to activate automatically if your primary sump pump loses power during an outage or fails. This is important for maintaining flood protection during power outages caused by severe weather.

    Key signs include the pump not turning on, unusual noises such as grinding or rattling, running nonstop, visible rust or corrosion, or an age over 7 to 10 years. Any of these could point to coming failure and a need for replacement.

    Most standard sump pump replacements can be completed within 2 to 4 hours. More complex installations, such as those requiring new pit excavation or major discharge line work, might take longer, up to a full day.
