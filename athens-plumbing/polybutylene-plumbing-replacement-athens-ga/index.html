    Frequently Asked Questions About Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Athens

    Most PEX replacements of polybutylene cost $5,000 to $12,000 for an average home. Copper costs more, and yard service lines often add $1,500 to $4,000.

    Gray or blue plastic pipe stamped PB2110 is the main sign. Cracked fittings are a warning. Poly often looks fine right up until it fails, so if your home was built between about 1978 and 1995 and has this pipe, replacement is recommended even without a leak yet.

    Both work well in Georgia homes. PEX bends, tolerates freezes and costs less. Copper is rigid, lasts a long time and tolerates sunlight and heat, and we will help you choose based on your home, your water and your budget.

    Look for gray, flexible plastic pipe at your water heater, under sinks or near the main shutoff, often stamped PB2110. It is most common in homes from the late 1970s to mid 1990s.

    Homes farther south often draw from deep limestone aquifers, so harder water and the occasional sulfur smell are common complaints there. Hard water does not cause poly to fail the way chlorine does. Scale builds inside. Scale buildup can reduce flow and add stress to pipe that is already brittle, so in hard water areas we often suggest adding a softener loop during the replacement.

    Every poly replacement we do comes with a written warranty on materials and labor. PEX makers add their own. Both warranties come in writing with the photos and permit record, all in one place for later.

    It is not a good DIY project. A full replacement needs a permit, pressure testing and code knowledge. Mistakes inside walls lead to hidden leaks, failed inspections or a voided PEX warranty, and those can easily cost more than a licensed crew would charge for the whole job.

    In most cases, yes, a plumbing permit is required for full repiping in Athens. We take care of it. We handle the application, the open-wall inspection and the final sign-off, leaving no paperwork for you.
