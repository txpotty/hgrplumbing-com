    Grinding Noise Repair in Dalton

    A metal-on-metal grind may mean a loose mount, a foreign object or worn impellers. We inspect the chamber, tighten the mounting ring and remove any debris, then run the unit empty to hear whether the motor itself is making the noise. Failing bearings are not worth fixing.

    Flange and Inlet Leak Repair in Dalton, GA

    The flange, dishwasher inlet, discharge tube and body are where leaks start. We trace the leak, reseal the flange with plumber's putty and replace gaskets or tubes as needed. A leak from the bottom of the unit usually means the internal seal has failed, and in that case replacement is the better option. It is not a repairable part.

    Leaking Disposal Fix in Dalton, GA

    A drip at the top usually means the sink flange putty has dried out. Side leaks are usually the discharge gasket or the dishwasher hose clamp.

    InSinkErator Disposal Repair in Dalton, GA

    InSinkErator makes the Badger line and the Evolution series, and we repair both.     InSinkErator and Other Brand Repairs in Dalton, GA

    InSinkErator, Waste King and Moen each use different mounts, so a flange leak is fixed with that brand's kit.     Preventive Care and Longevity in Dalton, GA

    Keep cold water running before, during and after use, and let it go about 15 seconds after the motor stops so the food clears the trap. Grease poured in with hot water coats the chamber and the pipe, while cold water keeps it solid so the impellers can break it up. Fibrous scraps jam it most. Throw out celery, corn husks and artichoke leaves.

    Noisy Disposal Repair in Dalton

    Grinding, rattling or a hum that sounds rough usually mean trouble inside the unit. Loose mounting rings rattle too. We check the mount first, then the chamber for debris, then listen to the motor with the chamber empty, because a motor that whines with nothing in it has bad bearings.

    Jam Clearing and Motor Repair in Dalton

    Bones, fruit pits and bottle caps wedge between the impeller lugs and the grind ring, and the motor stalls. We shut off power, insert a 1/4 inch hex key into the socket on the bottom of the unit and work it back and forth until the flywheel turns freely. Then the object comes out with pliers. Long stalls can overheat the motor, so we run it under load before we finish.
