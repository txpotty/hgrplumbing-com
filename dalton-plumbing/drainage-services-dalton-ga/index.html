    Frequently Asked Questions About Drainage Services in Dalton

    Drain clearing in Dalton usually runs $125 to $500 depending on the line, and hydro jetting runs $350 to $800. French drains cost more. Most residential French drain or downspout projects land between $2,000 and $8,000, depending on length, depth and how much hardscape we have to cut through or work around.

    We install and service Zoeller, Little Giant, Wayne and Liberty pumps. Cast iron models last longest. For homes that lose power in storms, we add a battery backup on its own float and a high-water alarm, so a dead primary pump or a power outage does not turn into a flooded floor.

    Mineral scale inside pipes narrows them and traps grease, hair and soap. Municipal water across much of the northern half of Georgia comes from rivers and reservoirs and tends to run soft, which is gentle on fixtures and water heaters. Older galvanized and cast iron drains are the worst, because their rough interior gives buildup something to cling to, and every new layer narrows the pipe a little more. Jetting strips it back.

    Snaking is the right first step for most single clogs. It is fast and inexpensive. Jetting wins for grease, scale and recurring roots since it cleans the whole wall.

    Simple downspout extensions are a reasonable DIY project. French drains are harder. A French drain or dry well needs correct slope, the right fabric and stone, and an outlet that will not dump water on a neighbor, and digging without an 811 locate risks hitting a gas or power line.

    A single fixture drain usually takes about an hour. Main lines take one to two hours. Camera work adds 30 to 60 minutes, and a buried or missing cleanout adds more.

    Most homes never need scheduled drain cleaning. Clean them when they slow down. Homes with root-prone sewers, old cast iron, or a kitchen that sends a lot of grease down the drain do better with a cleaning every 12 to 24 months, timed before the line slows to a crawl.

    Slow sinks and gurgling drains are the early signs. Sewer odor near a drain or cleanout often follows. In the yard, standing water a day after rain or washed-out mulch means the water has nowhere to go.
