    Recirculation Systems for Tankless Heaters in Dalton

    Tankless heaters can take a few extra seconds to get hot water to a far bathroom, since the burner fires only after it senses flow. A recirculation pump on a timer or a motion sensor keeps the hot line primed, so the wait at the farthest sink drops from a minute to a few seconds. It saves water too.

    Hard Water Protection for Tankless Units in Dalton, GA

    Calcium scale is the most common reason tankless heaters fail. Municipal water across much of the northern half of Georgia comes from rivers and reservoirs and tends to run fairly soft, which is gentle on fixtures and water heaters. Once scale coats the heat exchanger, efficiency drops and error codes start, and over time it can crack the exchanger and void the warranty. Flush it yearly. Service valves make it easy.

    Upgrading from Tank to Tankless in Dalton, GA

    Switching from a tank takes more than unbolting one and hanging the other. A 40-gallon tank burns about 40,000 BTU, but a whole-home tankless unit may need 150,000 to 199,000, so the meter line nearly always gets bigger. Venting changes too. Both are on the estimate.

    Tank to Tankless Conversions in Dalton

    Conversions in Dalton tend to run a full workday. We drain and remove the tank, upsize and test the gas, run a new vent through a wall or roof, then mount, plumb and fire the unit with service valves. You shower that night. The inspector comes after.

    Residential Tankless Water Heater Installation in Dalton

    Sizing a home unit depends on two figures: peak gallons per minute and temperature rise. With a shower at about 2 gallons per minute and a kitchen tap a bit under, two showers and a sink together need around 5 or 6 gallons per minute at the needed rise. It shortens the list quickly.
