    Expansion Tank Service in Johns Creek, GA

    A thermal expansion tank is the small steel tank on the cold line above the heater, and it gives heated water somewhere to go when a check valve or pressure reducer closes the system. Our tech checks the air charge with a tire gauge. Precharge should match your house water pressure. A waterlogged expansion tank sounds solid when you knock on it, and it lets pressure spike every time the burner fires.

    Electric Element Check in Johns Creek

    Electric tanks have two elements, usually 4,500 watts at 240 volts, and the lower one does most of the work and takes most of the scale. Each element gets an ohm reading. A failed lower element shows up as hot water that runs out halfway through a shower. We replace it with the matching wattage and voltage.

    Gas Venting Tune-Up in Johns Creek, GA

    Atmospheric gas tanks vent by draft alone, so the vent connector needs upward slope of at least a quarter inch per foot and tight joints with three screws each. We check draft at the hood. Lint on the flame arrestor of an FVIR tank can starve the burner and kill the pilot. We vacuum it clean.

    Maintenance Versus Replacement Costs in Johns Creek, GA

    A yearly flush costs a small fraction of a new installed tank, which often runs $1,400 to $3,000 or more depending on size and venting. You get a straight answer on which one is worth it. A tank over 10 to 12 years old with rust at the base is a replacement. Younger tanks with a sound liner are worth maintaining.

    Anode Rod Inspection and Replacement in Johns Creek, GA

    The anode rod is a sacrificial metal rod that corrodes so the steel tank does not. Private wells still serve a large share of rural Georgia homes, and iron, sediment, and low pH can shorten the life of pumps, tanks, and copper lines. We break the hex head loose, pull the rod and check how much bare wire shows. Six inches of bare wire means replace it.
