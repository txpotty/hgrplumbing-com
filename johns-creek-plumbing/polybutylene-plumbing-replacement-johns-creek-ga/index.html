    Frequently Asked Questions About Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Johns Creek

    PEX replacements usually run $4,000 to $10,000, and copper often runs $8,000 to $15,000 or more. Access changes the price. Size, fixtures and attic or crawlspace access set most of the cost, and you get a written quote after we see the house.

    Homes farther south often draw from deep limestone aquifers, so harder water and the occasional sulfur smell are common complaints there. Hard water does not cause poly to fail the way chlorine does. Scale builds inside. Scale buildup can reduce flow and add stress to pipe that is already brittle, so in hard water areas we often suggest adding a softener loop during the replacement.

    Properly installed PEX usually lasts 25 to 50 years. Copper can last longer. Type L copper can last 50 years or more in friendly water, and either one is a large step up from polybutylene.

    Both work well in Georgia homes. PEX bends, tolerates freezes and costs less. Copper is rigid, lasts a long time and tolerates sunlight and heat, and we will help you choose based on your home, your water and your budget.

    We provide a written workmanship warranty on the repipe, and pipe makers offer their own long-term warranties. You get the details before we start.

    Both are good choices. PEX usually costs less. PEX bends, handles freezes and movement and goes in quickly, and copper is stiff, heat tolerant and long proven, so it comes down to budget, water and preference.
