    Frequently Asked Questions About Well Pump Replacement in Johns Creek

    The cost of well pump replacement in Johns Creek, GA can vary widely, usually ranging from $1,500 to $5,000 or more. This price depends on factors like the type of pump, submersible or jet, well depth, horsepower and any additional components needed like pressure tanks or control boxes. We provide a detailed, upfront estimate after diagnosing your situation.

    In many areas, a plumbing or well permit is required for well pump replacement to confirm compliance with local building and health codes. HGR Plumbing handles all necessary permitting on your behalf, so your installation is fully compliant and safe.

    The lifespan of a well pump varies greatly depending on usage, water quality and pump type, but most last between 10 and 20 years. Regular maintenance and proper sizing can extend its life, but eventually wear and tear will require replacement.

    Jet pumps sit above ground and pull water up from shallower wells, usually less than 25 feet. Submersible pumps are placed directly inside the well casing, submerged in water, and are used for deeper wells, pushing water up to the surface. Each has its own applications and benefits.

    Well pump replacement is a complex job that involves high voltage electricity, heavy equipment and possibly deep well access. It requires specialized tools and experience for proper installation, electrical safety and compliance with local plumbing codes. It is highly recommended to hire a licensed professional to avoid injury, property damage or costly mistakes.

    New well pumps typically come with a manufacturer's warranty, usually ranging from 1 to 5 years, covering defects in materials and workmanship. HGR Plumbing also stands behind our installation work with our own labor warranty. We will discuss warranty details for your chosen pump during the quoting process.

    Shallow well pumps, or jet pumps, are designed for wells where the water level is usually 25 feet or less from the pump, using suction to draw water. Deep well pumps, or submersible pumps, are placed directly in the water near the bottom of the well and push water up, suited for depths over 25 feet. Choosing the correct type is critical for efficiency and performance.

    It is often a good idea to consider replacing your pressure tank at the same time as your well pump, especially if both are of similar age. A new pressure tank can improve the efficiency and lifespan of your new pump by reducing cycling. It also cuts labor costs to do both jobs at once.
