    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Cleaning in Roswell

    Small under-sink traps often start around $200 a visit. Large outdoor interceptors can pass $1,000 when they are full and the cap has hardened. Disposal is billed by the gallon at the receiving facility, so the gallons hauled drive most of the cost, and a 1,500-gallon tank fills a truck.

    Under-sink hydromechanical traps and in-ground gravity interceptors make up most of our work. Automatic grease removal units are on the list too. An AGRU uses a heater and skimmer wheel to move grease into a bin, and the bin, wheel and strainer all need regular cleaning.

    The terms get mixed up constantly. Generally a trap is the small unit indoors, often under 100 gallons. Interceptor usually means the large tank in the ground outside, often 500 gallons and up, and codes size the two in different ways.

    Watch the floor sinks during dish rush. Standing water in a floor sink is the first hint. Other signs include a rancid smell near the dish station, grease showing up in the cleanout nearest the street, and drains that gurgle after the dish machine empties.

    FOG stands for fats, oils and grease. It pours as a liquid and sets up hard once it cools. Inside a sewer pipe it builds up in layers on the walls, traps food and wipes, and can shrink the opening to the size of a pencil.

    It is the most common pumping standard. When grease and solids reach a quarter of the depth, it is time to pump. Past that point the water moves through too fast for grease to separate, so grease rides out the outlet even though the trap looks like it is working.

    Two things, a full pump-out and a trap that drains properly when we leave. We do not skim and call it done. Should a problem tied to our work show up later, the crew returns to fix it, and the depth readings on the sheet show how we left the trap.

    Under-sink traps take 30 to 60 minutes. Outdoor interceptors run one to three hours. The long end is for big tanks with a hard grease cap, where the crew has to break up the cap with a pressure wand before the suction hose can move it.
