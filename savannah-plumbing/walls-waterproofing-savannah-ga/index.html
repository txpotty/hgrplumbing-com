    How We Keep Water Out of Walls in Savannah, GA

    Waterproofing for Older Homes in Savannah, GA

    Houses built before modern drainage rules often have no footing drain at all, or a clay tile drain that collapsed decades ago. A large share of Georgia homes were built during the postwar and suburban growth decades, with copper supply lines and cast iron or early plastic drains. Fieldstone and old brick foundations need breathable products. Sealing soft old brick with a rigid coating traps water inside it and makes the face spall off over a few seasons, so we use lime-compatible or vapor-open coatings and pair them with interior drain tile. We repoint failed mortar joints before sealing.

    Foundation Crack Repair in Savannah

    Crack injection takes about two hours per crack. We set injection ports every 8 to 12 inches along the crack, seal the surface with epoxy paste, and pump in resin from the bottom port up until it pushes out the next port. Polyurethane stays flexible if the wall moves a little, and epoxy bonds the two sides back together. The surface paste gets ground off once it cures.

    Shower Wall Waterproofing in Savannah

    Tile and grout are not waterproof. Water passes through grout every time you shower, so the layer behind the tile, a membrane over cement board or a foam board system, is what actually keeps the studs dry. We tear out failed tile, install new backer, and apply Schluter Kerdi, RedGard or Hydro Ban at the thickness the manufacturer calls for. Corners, niches and valve holes get extra fabric or preformed pieces.

    Drainage System Integration in Savannah, GA

    Water has to go somewhere once a wall is sealed. Exterior footing drains, interior drain tile and surface French drains all do the same job at different depths. We match the drain to where the water comes from, which might be a high water table, a yard that slopes toward the house, or a downspout emptying right at the foundation. Downspouts get extended at least four feet out.

    Commercial Wall Waterproofing in Savannah

    Restaurant kitchens, gym locker rooms and salon wash stations put water on walls all day. We waterproof commercial showers, mop sink walls and restroom wet walls with membranes rated for constant water exposure, and we repair the supply and drain piping behind them when we find a leak during demo. We can work after hours.

    Shower Pan Leak Repair and Waterproofing in Savannah, GA

    Wet baseboards outside the shower or a stain on the ceiling below usually point to the pan. We plug the drain, fill the pan with an inch or two of water, and mark the level and wait 24 hours. A drop means a leak. Rebuilding means a mortar pre-slope of a quarter inch per foot, a new PVC liner or bonded membrane, and a clamping drain tightened onto the liner. The new pan gets its own flood test before tile.

    Crawl Space Vapor Barriers in Savannah

    Bare dirt in a crawl space releases gallons of water vapor a day into the air under your floor. A 10 mil or heavier polyethylene barrier, overlapped a foot at every seam and taped, cuts that off and keeps the joists and subfloor dry. We run it up the foundation walls and fasten it with termination bar. Cheap fix, big difference.

    Exterior Foundation Waterproofing in Savannah

    Exterior waterproofing means digging down to the footing and coating the outside of the wall. It is the most reliable fix for a leaking basement, because the water never touches the block or concrete. We apply a rubberized asphalt or polymer membrane, add a dimple board to protect it, and set perforated drain pipe in gravel along the footing to carry the water away from the wall. Much of Georgia sits on dense red clay that swells when wet and shrinks in drought, which can stress slab plumbing and sewer laterals over time. We backfill after the drain is tested.

    Wall Waterproofing Near Me in Savannah, GA

    Water in a wall does not wait for a convenient week. We keep appointments open for active leaks, and when a pipe is still dripping inside the wall we can shut it off, open the drywall and start drying the cavity that same day. Most cavities dry out in three to five days.

    Moisture meters and thermal cameras ride on every truck. That lets us tell you what is wrong before we leave, including where the water is coming from and which repair fits, instead of scheduling a second trip just to diagnose. Quotes come in writing.
