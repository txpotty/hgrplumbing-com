    Which Sump Pump Fits Your Savannah, GA Home

    Battery Backup Sump Pump Installation in Savannah

    Coastal and low-lying parts of Georgia face flood risk during hurricane season, and saturated ground can push water into crawlspaces and basements. A primary pump on household current does nothing once the breaker panel goes dark. We add a 12-volt backup pump on its own float, mounted a few inches above the primary, with a deep-cycle battery and a charger that tests itself. It also covers a stuck primary switch. The alarm will tell you which one is running.

    Yard Drainage Pit Pumps in Savannah, GA

    Yard drainage pits and window well pumps can sit dry from fall to spring and then seize the first time a big storm fills them. We clean the debris out and install a pump built to handle grit. We route the outlet away from the house. A bucket test every spring catches problems early.

    Cast Iron Submersible Pumps in Savannah

    Cast iron sheds motor heat into the water around it far better than a plastic housing does, which is a big part of why a Zoeller M53 or Liberty 250 outlasts a hardware-store pump. Most swaps take one to two hours. Before we set it, we make sure the float can swing freely without catching the wall.

    Pedestal Pumps for Shallow Pits in Savannah, GA

    Older basements sometimes have a narrow, shallow pit poured right into the floor, and a pedestal pump is often the only thing that fits without breaking concrete. We swap the pump, replace the float rod and stops, and run new 1.5-inch PVC to the check valve. They usually cost less than a submersible.

    Sizing a Submersible Sump Pump in Savannah

    Horsepower matters less than people think. A third-horse pump moves plenty of water for most homes, as long as the vertical lift to the discharge is under about 15 feet. Before we choose, we read the pump curve at your real discharge height. Oversize it and it short cycles.

    High-Capacity Sump Pump Upgrades in Savannah

    If your pump runs every 30 seconds during a storm and still loses ground, it is undersized for the water coming in. We time the cycles and measure how fast the pit refills. The rest is simple arithmetic. Often a 1/2 or 3/4 HP pump with more GPM at your lift, plus a 2-inch discharge line, solves it.

    Submersible Sump Pump Replacement in Savannah

    A submersible pump sits at the bottom of the pit, sealed in an oil-filled housing, and it runs quieter than anything else you can put down there. Out comes the old pump, out comes the muck at the bottom of the basin, and the new one goes in on a brick to stay clear of silt. The check valve gets replaced at the same time. Skip that part and the pump short cycles.

    Wayne Sump Pump Swaps in Savannah

    Wayne makes everything from a thermoplastic quarter-horse pump at the hardware store to cast iron models with vertical switches, and the cheap ones are what we replace most. The usual failure is a burned-out motor or a float that hangs up on the cord. You get a matching Wayne or a sturdier cast iron model, your call. Figure on a couple of hours.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Sump Pump Replacement in Savannah

    Expect $500 to $1,500 for the pump and labor. A new basin or a long discharge reroute pushes the number up, sometimes by several hundred dollars on its own. We quote it after seeing the pit.

    The primary pump runs on household current and does the daily work. The backup, battery or water powered, sits a few inches higher and only runs if the primary fails, loses power or cannot keep up with a heavy inflow. It is cheap insurance.

    A pump that hums without moving water, rattles, runs nonstop or shows rust on the housing is near the end. Age matters too. Once a pump passes 7 to 10 years it is due, even if it still kicks on during a bucket test.

    Like-for-like pump swaps usually do not need a permit in most jurisdictions. Adding a circuit or moving the discharge line can require one, and every city handles it a little differently. We check the local rule before we start.

    Three to five years from most manufacturers. Our labor warranty covers the install.

    Manufacturer coverage on the pump usually runs 1 to 3 years for plastic models and up to 5 for cast iron. Our labor warranty sits on top of that. You will see the terms in writing.

    Listen for grinding or rattling, and watch for a pump that cycles every minute or fails to start in heavy rain. Corrosion on the housing points the same way. Past 7 to 10 years, replace it before storm season.
