    Jetting a broken pipe can make the break worse. The camera always goes in first. The video shows the pipe material, the condition of each joint, and any cracks, offsets or sags, which tells us the nozzle and pressure to use or whether we should not jet that line at all.

    Scheduled Jetting Maintenance in Savannah

    Properties with chronic drain issues benefit from scheduled jetting. Commercial kitchens often jet quarterly. Homes with root-prone sewers usually jet every year or two, and we keep a camera record from each visit so you can see whether the roots are coming back slower, faster or the same.

    Chemical-Free Drain Cleaning in Savannah, GA

    Hydrojetting uses only water, so nothing caustic goes into your pipes or septic tank. Chemical cleaners can damage pipe. Jetting removes the buildup physically, and the debris flushes to the sewer or septic tank as normal waste, so there is nothing left in the pipe to eat at joints or harm the bacteria a septic system depends on.

    Preventive Hydrojet Maintenance in Savannah

    Preventive jetting makes sense when the same line clogs more than once a year. Camera findings set the interval. A line with heavy grease might need jetting every six months, while a sewer with a few root entry points might only need it every 18 to 24 months, and the footage tells us which.

    Hydrojetting vs Snaking Cost Comparison in Savannah, GA

    Snaking a main line typically costs $200 to $450, while residential hydrojetting usually runs $400 to $900 with a camera inspection. Jetting costs more up front. Because jetting removes the buildup that causes repeat clogs, many homeowners spend less over a few years than they would on repeated snaking visits for the same line.

    Hydrojetting vs. Snaking in Savannah, GA

    Snaking pokes a hole through a clog. A jet scours the entire pipe wall. For a single clog in an otherwise clean line, snaking is cheaper and works fine, but for a line that clogs every few months, jetting removes the buildup that keeps catching debris.

    Kitchen Grease Line Jetting in Savannah

    Kitchen drains collect grease that cools and hardens along the pipe wall, especially in long horizontal runs under a slab. A cable cuts a channel through it. Jetting with a spinning nozzle strips the grease from the full circumference so it flushes downstream instead of re-forming a plug at the next fitting.
