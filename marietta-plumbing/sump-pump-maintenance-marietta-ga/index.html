    Commercial Building Sump Pumps in Marietta, GA

    Parking garages, mechanical rooms and loading docks often drain to large pits with pumps rated at a horsepower or more. Those units need more frequent checks than a house pump.     Submersible Sump Pump Maintenance in Marietta

    Submersible pumps sit at the bottom of the pit, which keeps them quiet but hides the problems. We lift the unit out to look at the seals and the cord. A cracked cord usually means a new pump. The intake screen gets cleaned, and we look over the housing for corrosion, since pumps sitting in standing water for years rust from the bottom up.

    Annual Sump Pump Check-Ups in Marietta

    Once a year is the minimum. Each yearly visit covers the motor, the impeller, the float and the discharge line. After that, we fill the basin and time the drawdown, since a slow pump-out is the first hint of a tired impeller or a restricted line.

    Crawlspace and Window Well Pumps in Marietta, GA

    Not every sump sits in a basement. Crawlspaces and deep window wells often have small pumps that clear runoff after a storm. Our plumber clears the screen, tests the float and traces the discharge to daylight or an approved drain, since a line that ends under the house only relocates the water. Northern and higher-elevation areas see more frozen-pipe calls than the south, where a true hard freeze may only come every few winters.

    Wayne Sump Pump Maintenance in Marietta, GA

    Some Wayne models draw water through a top suction intake, which helps them avoid pulling in the sludge that settles at the bottom of a pit. We check that intake and the float mechanism for buildup. Any brand can rattle its discharge fittings loose over time. Easy fix.

    Maintenance Versus Replacement Cost in Marietta, GA

    A new sump pump, installed, often runs $400 to $1,200 depending on horsepower, pit work and whether you add a battery backup. A maintenance visit costs a small slice of that number. With regular service, seven to ten years is typical. We will tell you plainly whether yours has years left or is ready to retire.

    Pre-Storm Season Pump Testing in Marietta

    Coastal and low-lying parts of Georgia face flood risk during hurricane season, and saturated ground can push water into crawlspaces and basements. We test the pump a few weeks before the wet season by filling the pit with a hose until the float trips. We time the drawdown and listen for bearing noise. That leaves time to replace a weak pump before the first big storm, not during it.

    Float Switch Inspection in Marietta, GA

    The float switch tells the pump when to start and stop, and a float with too little travel between on and off makes the pump short cycle dozens of times an hour. Our plumber watches the float rise and drop through a complete cycle. Then we set the on and off points so the pump runs longer, less often.
