    What a Tank Service Visit Covers in Marietta, GA

    Standard Tank Tune-Up in Marietta

    A standard tune-up starts with the fuel off and the cold supply closed, then a full drain through the bottom valve until the water runs clear. Figure on half an hour or a bit more for the drain. We check the heating elements or burner, read the thermostat setting and look at every fitting on top of the tank for green crust or drips. Small leaks at the dielectric nipples get noted on your report.

    Anode Upgrades for Odor in Marietta, GA

    A rotten egg smell from the hot side only is almost always sulfate-reducing bacteria reacting with a magnesium anode. Swapping the rod usually fixes it. We put in an aluminum-zinc or powered anode and sanitize the tank. Powered anodes never wear out and draw very little electricity.

    Maintenance Versus Replacement Costs in Marietta, GA

    A yearly flush costs a small fraction of a new installed tank, which often runs $1,400 to $3,000 or more depending on size and venting. We tell you which one makes sense. Past 10 to 12 years, rust at the base or a seam leak means replacement. Younger tanks with a sound liner are worth maintaining.

    Emergency Tank Troubleshooting in Marietta

    No hot water at all is usually a pilot, a gas valve, a tripped high-limit or a dead element. Most of those are quick fixes. Water on the floor is a different story, and the first question we ask is whether it is coming from a fitting on top or from under the jacket. Leaks from under the jacket mean the tank itself has failed.

    Emergency Tank Inspections in Marietta

    Sudden changes call for a look the same day: a tank that suddenly rumbles, hot water that turns brown, a puddle forming under the pan, or a relief valve that will not stop dripping. We diagnose first. Sometimes the fix is a flush and a new drain valve, and sometimes the tank is leaking from the liner and nothing will save it.

    Rheem and Bradford White Tank Service in Marietta

    Rheem, Bradford White, AO Smith and State make most of the tanks in local garages, and each has quirks a tech should know before opening a drain valve. On some models the anode sits under a plastic top cap. Bradford White often uses a combo anode in the hot outlet nipple. Our trucks carry sockets for each brand.

    Sediment Flush and Descaling in Marietta

    Calcium carbonate drops out of hard water every time the tank heats, and over a year it can harden into chunks the size of a thumbnail. That is what makes the popping noise. When a plain flush will not move them, we use a descaling solution rated for potable systems, let it sit and then flush it all out. A clean bottom puts less heat stress on the seam.

    Electric Element Check in Marietta

    Electric tanks have two elements, usually 4,500 watts at 240 volts, and the lower one does most of the work and takes most of the scale. Each element gets an ohm reading. A failed lower element shows up as hot water that runs out halfway through a shower. We replace it with the matching wattage and voltage.
