    What Goes Into a Grease Trap Installation in Valdosta, GA

    Hydro-Mechanical Grease Trap Installation in Valdosta, GA

    Hydromechanical traps use baffles and a vented flow control to pull grease out of water moving at a set rate, usually 20 to 100 gallons per minute. You can put one under a sink, beside a dish station or in a pit in the slab. The flow control is not optional. Skip it and a full sink drain overruns the rating, which sends grease right past the baffles. We look for units certified to PDI G-101 or ASME A112.14.3.

    Grease Trap Brands We Install in Valdosta

    Zurn, Watts, Schier and Endura cover most of what we set in commercial kitchens, plus precast concrete tanks for the big outdoor interceptors. Each brand has its own lid gasket and baffle layout. Parts availability matters. We prefer models whose lids and gaskets are stocked nearby, because waiting a week on a part means waiting a week to reopen.

    Commercial Grease Trap Installation in Valdosta

    A commercial kitchen with a three-compartment sink, a pre-rinse station and two floor sinks can push 50 gallons a minute of greasy water at the dinner rush. Our sizing starts from your peak flow, the worst ten minutes of the night, rather than a daily average. Undersized traps are the usual failure. The install covers setting the unit, piping the inlet and outlet, and adding the vented flow control the maker specifies.

    Residential Grease Interceptor Setup in Valdosta, GA

    Homes rarely need a grease trap, though some do. Licensed home bakeries and houses on old clay laterals that keep clogging are the usual cases. A compact under-sink unit handles the load, and a homeowner can clean it in a few minutes. Check with your local health office first.

    In-Ground Gravity Interceptor Installation in Valdosta, GA

    Buffets, grocery delis and kitchens doing several hundred covers a night usually end up with an in-ground gravity interceptor of 1,000 gallons or more. We excavate, set the tank level on compacted gravel, and tie in the inlet and outlet. Much of Georgia sits on dense red clay that swells when wet and shrinks in drought, which can stress slab plumbing and sewer laterals over time. We plan the dig and the backfill around what the soil report and the open hole actually show us. Risers bring the lids to grade.

    Replacement and Upgrade Services in Valdosta, GA

    Steel traps from decades back rust through at the baffle seams, and by then the outlet is usually passing grease straight to the sewer. If your seats or menu grew since opening, the swap is the time to step up to a bigger GPM rating.
