    What Our Gas Water Heater Service Calls Cover in Atlanta, GA

    Pilot Light and Ignition Service in Atlanta

    No hot water and a dark pilot window usually point to the ignition side of the heater. We pull the pilot assembly and look at the thermocouple tip, the pilot orifice and the piezo igniter wire. A thermocouple that tests weak on the millivolt meter will not hold the gas valve's magnet open, so the pilot drops out a minute after you relight it by the label. Most trucks carry the common pilot kits.

    Burner Assembly and Venting Repair in Atlanta

    Rust scale on the burner tray is a sign the flue baffle or the tank top is shedding. The burner comes out, the ports get cleared and the flame arrestor gets a look. Then the vent gets checked from the draft hood to the roof, because a flue that is loose, sagging or capped by a bird nest can push carbon monoxide back into your Atlanta home. A smoke pencil shows the draft.

    Repairing Older Gas Water Heaters in Atlanta

    Older tanks with standing pilots and no vapor sensor are still out there in Atlanta, mostly in basements and utility closets. Their parts are simple, and a universal thermocouple fits nearly all of them. We can keep a pre-2003 heater running when the tank is sound, but we also check the draft hood, the flue connector and the gas cock, since those fittings on an old install are often original and corroded. Some old setups lack a sediment trap. We add one during the repair.

    Bradford White & Rheem Gas Water Heater Repair in Atlanta, GA

    Bradford White and Rheem tanks make up a big share of what we repair in Atlanta, GA. Most Rheem gas units run a White-Rodgers or Honeywell valve with a blinking status LED. Bradford White uses its own sealed combustion design with a lint screen at the base and a thermal switch, and it needs a specific kit when the pilot assembly goes bad. Both kits ride on the truck. Using factory parts keeps your tank warranty valid.

    Flammable Vapor Sensor Repair in Atlanta

    Since about 2003, most residential gas tanks have been built with a flammable vapor ignition resistant combustion chamber. A flame arrestor screen at the base lets air in and keeps flame from getting out. When lint, dust or pet hair coats that screen, the burner starves, the chamber overheats and a thermal switch shuts the whole heater down, sometimes several times a week. We clean the screen and the chamber. A tripped thermal switch gets replaced.

    Commercial Gas Water Heater Repair in Atlanta, GA

    Restaurants and salons in Atlanta, GA burn through hot water faster than any house, and a down heater stops the business. Most commercial tanks hold 75 to 100 gallons and run far higher BTU inputs than a home unit. We work on those larger atmospheric and power vent units, including the electronic controls, the burner manifolds and the piping that ties two tanks together on one supply. Every hour down matters. We can often schedule the repair before opening or after close.

    All Major Gas Water Heater Brands Repaired in Atlanta

    Every brand label we see in garages and closets uses one of a handful of gas control designs. Rheem and Ruud share most parts, and State shares a lot with A.O. Smith. Bradford White runs its own control system, so we stock those separately. Knowing which family a heater belongs to lets the tech bring the right valve, thermocouple or igniter on the first trip instead of ordering parts and coming back two days later. A photo of the rating plate helps us prep.

    Propane Water Heater Repair in Atlanta

    Propane and natural gas heaters look the same but burn at different pressures. A propane tank needs its own orifice and a valve set for roughly 11 inches of water column. Natural gas runs closer to 7 inches, so a heater converted badly, or a regulator that drifted, gives you soot, a yellow flame and a pilot that keeps going out for no obvious reason. We check pressure with a manometer. A bad conversion gets corrected with the right parts.
