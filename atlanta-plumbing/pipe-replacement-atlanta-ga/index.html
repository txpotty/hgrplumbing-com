    Repiping and Pipe Replacement Options in Atlanta, GA

    Commercial Pipe Replacement in Atlanta

    Commercial buildings often have larger supply lines and more fixtures. Work has to fit around your hours. We plan pipe replacement in phases whenever the layout allows it, so only part of the building is offline at a time. Night work available.

    Polybutylene Pipe Replacement in Atlanta

    Polybutylene is gray or blue plastic installed from roughly the late 1970s to mid 1990s. It degrades from the inside. Failures tend to be sudden splits at fittings rather than slow drips, and some insurers refuse to cover homes that still have it. Replace it.

    Pipe Replacement for Multi-Unit Buildings in Atlanta

    Apartments and condos share risers. Shared lines mean shared problems. We plan riser replacement in phases, one stack at a time, notify tenants ahead of shutoffs, and test each section before moving on. Organized work.

    Slab Leak Pipe Replacement in Atlanta

    Rerouting is often better than digging. Breaking a slab is messy and costly. We run new pipe through the attic or walls to bypass the leaking line under the concrete, and usually costs less than repairing in place. Fast too.

    Galvanized Pipe Replacement in Atlanta, GA

    Galvanized steel was common before the 1960s. Inside, rust builds once the coating fails. Over decades the opening shrinks, pressure drops and the water turns brown after sitting, and the threads where pipes join are often the first to leak. Replacing it is the only fix.

    Copper Pipe Replacement in Atlanta, GA

    Copper still has fans. It handles heat well and lasts decades. We install Type L copper for most interior supply lines, sweat or press the joints, and support every run with hangers at the spacing code requires, so the new pipe does not rattle or sag. Costs more than PEX.

    Whole-Home Re-Piping in Atlanta

    A whole-home repipe replaces every supply line from the main shutoff to each fixture, including the water heater connections. Plan on a few days for a typical house. We run new trunk lines through the attic or crawlspace, branch down to fixtures through small wall openings, and keep water on at night whenever we can. Then patching.

    Copper Pinhole Leak Replacement in Atlanta, GA

    Pinhole leaks start small. First a damp spot, then drywall damage. They usually mean the copper wall has thinned from corrosion, so finding one often means others are close. Repipe or patch, we explain.
