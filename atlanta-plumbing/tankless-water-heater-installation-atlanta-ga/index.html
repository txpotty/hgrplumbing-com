    What Goes Into a Tankless Conversion in Atlanta, GA

    Upgrading from Tank to Tankless in Atlanta, GA

    Switching from a tank is more than a swap. A 40-gallon tank burns about 40,000 BTU, but a whole-home tankless unit may need 150,000 to 199,000, so the meter line nearly always gets bigger. Venting changes too. We plan both on the quote.

    Tankless Water Heater Relocation in Atlanta

    Moving the heater is common on a tankless job because the old tank spot is not always the best place. A garage or outside wall near the gas meter can shorten the gas run and simplify venting, but it may lengthen the hot water run to the bathrooms. We weigh both. Long hot runs may call for recirculation.

    Hard Water Protection for Tankless Units in Atlanta, GA

    Calcium scale is what kills most tankless units early. Private wells still serve a large share of rural Georgia homes, and iron, sediment, and low pH can shorten the life of pumps, tanks, and copper lines. Scale coats the heat exchanger, cuts efficiency and triggers error codes, and left alone it can crack the exchanger and end the warranty claim before it starts. Flush it yearly. Service valves make it easy.

    Residential Tankless Water Heater Installation in Atlanta

    Sizing a home unit depends on two figures: peak gallons per minute and temperature rise. A shower head flows about 2 gallons per minute and a kitchen faucet a bit less, so a house that runs two showers and a sink together needs roughly 5 to 6 gallons at the rise your inlet water calls for. It shortens the list quickly.

    Residential Tankless System Upgrades in Atlanta

    Upgrading from a tank in Atlanta starts with a look at what you have now: tank size, vent type, gas line size and where the unit sits. In some homes the old flue gets capped and the new unit vents out a side wall in PVC, which is simpler and opens the old chase. Other homes need a longer run. We spell that out on the quote.

    Propane Tankless Water Heater Installation in Atlanta

    Propane homes in Atlanta can run tankless units too, using a model built or converted for propane. Since propane runs at different pressure from natural gas, the regulator, line sizing and gas valve must all match, and the supplier may need to swap the tank regulator. A small tank can run short.
