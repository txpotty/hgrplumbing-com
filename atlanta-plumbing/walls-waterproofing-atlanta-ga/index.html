    How We Keep Water Out of Walls in Atlanta, GA

    Interior Walls Waterproofing in Atlanta, GA

    Interior drain tile handles water that already got through the wall. We cut a channel in the slab along the perimeter, lay perforated pipe in washed stone, and tie it into a sump pit so the pump can push the water outside. The wall still gets wet. The difference is that the water no longer ends up on your floor. This route costs less than digging outside and works where a driveway or deck blocks the wall.

    Waterproofing Costs vs. Repeat Water Damage in Atlanta, GA

    Paying once beats paying every two years. Waterproofing a shower wall usually lands in the low thousands, while replacing drywall, cabinets and flooring again and again costs far more, and mold remediation on top of that can double the bill. Exterior block sealing depends on wall length. We write up each option with its price so you can compare.

    Foundation Crack Repair & Sealing in Atlanta

    Hairline cracks in poured concrete walls leak more water than their size suggests. We inject them with low-pressure polyurethane foam, which expands to fill the crack from the inside face to the soil side, or with epoxy when the crack also needs structural bonding. Horizontal cracks are different. A horizontal crack often signals soil pressure pushing the wall in, so we check for movement with a level before we seal anything. Vertical cracks are usually a one-visit job.

    Plumbing Leak Behind Walls in Atlanta

    A drip from a shower valve cartridge, a sweated copper stub or a loose drain slip nut can soak a wall cavity for months before paint bubbles. We find it with a moisture meter, a thermal camera that picks up the cooler wet area, and a pressure test on the supply lines. Then we cut a clean access hole. We replace the leaking fitting or pipe section. We pull wet insulation and run fans until the cavity reads dry.

    Waterproofing for Older Homes in Atlanta, GA

    Houses built before modern drainage rules often have no footing drain at all, or a clay tile drain that collapsed decades ago. Houses from the late seventies through the mid nineties may still have polybutylene supply pipe, which is known to fail and is worth replacing. Fieldstone and old brick foundations need breathable products. Sealing soft old brick with a rigid coating traps water inside it and makes the face spall off over a few seasons, so we use lime-compatible or vapor-open coatings and pair them with interior drain tile. We repoint failed mortar joints before sealing.

    Crawl Space Vapor Barriers in Atlanta

    Bare dirt in a crawl space releases gallons of water vapor a day into the air under your floor. A 10 mil or heavier polyethylene barrier, overlapped a foot at every seam and taped, cuts that off and keeps the joists and subfloor dry. We run it up the foundation walls and fasten it with termination bar. It is a cheap fix that makes a big difference.

    Laundry Room and Utility Wall Waterproofing in Atlanta, GA

    Rubber washing machine hoses burst. We swap them for braided stainless hoses, put a drain pan under the washer with a line to a floor drain where one is available, and install a quarter-turn shutoff or an automatic leak valve on the supply box. Water heaters get a pan too. Behind the heater we can install moisture-resistant board and seal it.

    Foundation Crack Repair in Atlanta

    Crack injection takes about two hours per crack. We set injection ports every 8 to 12 inches along the crack, seal the surface with epoxy paste, and pump in resin from the bottom port up until it pushes out the next port. Polyurethane stays flexible if the wall moves a little, and epoxy bonds the two sides back together. The surface paste gets ground off once it cures.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Walls Waterproofing in Atlanta

    Crack injection runs about $300 to $800 per crack. Prices vary with access. Each quote is itemized after we inspect.

    Interior systems manage water after it has come through the wall, using drain tile under the slab edge and a sump pump. Exterior waterproofing stops water before it reaches the wall, which means digging down to the footing and applying a membrane to the outside face. The exterior route costs more.

    Most systems we install should go 20 to 30 years or longer. Life span comes down to how it was installed, what method was used and what the soil around the house is like. The sump pump will need replacing well before that. Most pumps last seven to ten years.

    Schluter Kerdi, Custom RedGard and Laticrete Hydro Ban cover most of our showers. Sheet membranes like Kerdi suit a fast schedule since there is no cure time, while liquids like RedGard and Hydro Ban go on in two coats and need to dry between them. Each manufacturer's install steps are followed exactly.

    Xypex, Tremco, Sika and Henry products show up on a lot of our jobs, covering crystalline products, elastomeric membranes and sealants. The product depends on the wall. Poured concrete takes crystalline coatings well, while block often needs an elastomeric membrane or a drainage board.

    Done right with good materials, a waterproofing system lasts 20 to 50 years. The range is wide because soil movement, the water table and the quality of the original install all play a part, and a crack that opens later can let water around even a good membrane. Over time, drain tile can fill with silt.
