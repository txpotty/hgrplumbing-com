    Slab Leak Warning Signs Every Georgia Homeowner Should Know

    By Aaron Mitchell · October 15, 2026 · 5 min read

    What a Slab Leak Is

    A slab leak is a break in a water or drain line that runs under or through the concrete floor of your house. You cannot see it. Most supply lines under a slab are soft copper or PEX, and the first failure is usually a pinhole about the size of a pencil point. Years of shifting soil and seasonal temperature change keep stressing that buried line.

    Attic and crawlspace temperatures climb quickly in summer, which speeds up wear on supply lines, drain pans, and any plastic piping run through those spaces.

    Concrete hides the damage, so a leak can run for months before anyone notices. By that point the water has often soaked the padding and the subfloor. We have pulled up laminate where the slab underneath was wet from one wall to the other, and the homeowner only called because the bill doubled. Early means a smaller hole in the floor.

    The southern coastal plain has sandier soil and a higher water table, so drainage and root intrusion are bigger concerns there than shifting.

    Soil under a house expands when wet and shrinks when dry, and every cycle puts a little more load on pipe that is locked in concrete. Copper wears thin where it rubs against rebar or gravel. Hard water speeds that wear from the inside. PVC drain lines under the slab crack at the joints more often than along the pipe.

    Signs You Can Check Yourself

    A Water Bill That Jumps for No Reason

    Pull up the last six months of bills and compare the gallons, not the dollars. A jump of 20 or 30 percent with no new pool or sprinkler schedule deserves a look. Then do the meter test. Turn off every faucet and appliance, note the reading, and leave the water untouched for two hours. A meter that moved means water is leaving the system somewhere between the meter and your fixtures, and under the slab is a common spot.

    Warm Spots on the Floor

    A hot water line leaking under tile will heat the slab above it. Walk the house barefoot in the morning. What you want to find is one warm patch while the rest of the tile is cool, usually close to a bathroom or the kitchen. Pets find these spots before people do, so watch where the dog likes to lie in winter.

    Running Water With Everything Off

    Stand in a quiet house at night and listen near the floor. A hiss or a faint rush with no fixture running points to pressure escaping from a supply line. Some leaks are loud enough to hear. Others need an acoustic listening device, which is what our techs use to pinpoint the spot within a foot or two.

    New Cracks in Walls or Flooring

    Water under a slab washes out fine soil and lets the concrete settle unevenly. You may see a crack that runs across a room in a straight line. Doors that suddenly stick are another clue. Drywall cracks that start at the corner of a door frame and angle upward tell us the slab moved, and a leak is one of the reasons it does.

    Musty Smell or Mildew at the Baseboards

    Carpet over a leak stays damp and starts to smell like a basement. Look for dark staining along the baseboard or tack strip. Mildew low on a wall with no fixture behind it usually means the moisture is coming up from the slab. A moisture meter confirms it in seconds.

    Getting a Slab Leak Found and Fixed

    Do not start breaking concrete on a guess. Our techs pressure test each line first, then use acoustic and thermal gear to mark the exact location. From there the choice is usually between a spot repair through the slab and a reroute through the attic or walls. The right choice depends mostly on pipe age and how many leaks the house has already had.
