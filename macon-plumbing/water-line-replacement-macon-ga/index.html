    Type L vs. Type K Copper in Macon, GA

    The thickest wall belongs to Type K, the usual choice underground. Type L is lighter and mostly goes indoors. Some codes allow L for burial, but we stick with K for service lines because a thicker wall buys more years against corrosive soil and stray rocks. Same fittings, different pipe.

    Traditional Water Line Excavation in Macon

    Sometimes the open trench wins. A collapsed line, a route with sharp bends, or solid rock can rule out trenchless. We cut the sod and set it aside, dig to depth with a mini excavator or by hand near other utilities, set the pipe on clean bedding, and backfill in layers with compaction so the trench does not sink after the first good rain. Sod goes back on top.

    Copper Water Line Installation in Macon

    Type K copper is the thick-wall grade made for burial. It handles high street pressure without complaint. We bed it in sand, keep it away from sharp rock, and sleeve it where it passes through a foundation wall, because concrete and copper rubbing together for twenty years eventually wear a hole. PEX is cheaper.

    PEX Service Lines for Long Runs in Macon

    We buy PEX in long coils. That makes it the easy pick when the meter sits out at the road and the house is set far back, because one coil covers the whole run without a joint. Fewer joints underground means fewer places a leak can start, and it is the joints that usually fail first.

    PEX Water Line Installation in Macon, GA

    PEX bends around corners, so a typical service run needs no fittings between the meter and the house. Before a cold night, disconnecting hoses, covering hose bibs, and letting a faucet drip are usually enough to prevent a burst line. A frozen PEX line often survives where copper would crack. We still bury it below the frost depth the local code sets, and we keep it out of direct sun at the meter box, since UV light breaks it down. Short exposure only.

    Water Line Materials We Install in Macon, GA

    Our trucks carry Type K copper, PEX-a and HDPE. Each has a place. Copper suits short runs and high pressure, PEX handles long runs with bends, and HDPE is the usual pick for trenchless pulls and bores, where we need a tough, fused pipe that can be dragged a long way. All of it is listed for drinking water, carrying the NSF 61 listing.
