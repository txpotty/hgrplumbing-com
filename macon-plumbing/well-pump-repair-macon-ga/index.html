    Grundfos Well Pump Repair in Macon

    Grundfos SQ and SQE pumps have built-in electronics that protect the motor and report faults. Most Grundfos calls involve a sensor or a drive fault. HGR Plumbing reads the fault code, checks the wiring and tests the pressure sensor before replacing anything in a Macon, GA well. Electronics fail less often than people think.

    Deep Well Pump Repair in Macon

    Deep wells get submersible pumps. They push water up instead of pulling it, and they run quiet for years, but motors, impellers and wire splices still fail eventually. We pull the pump with the right equipment, test it and replace only what failed. Heat-shrink splice kits and a torque arrestor protect the new work in your Macon, GA well.

    Low Water Pressure Repair in Macon

    Weak pressure on a well is not always the pump. A clogged sediment filter, a pressure switch set too low or a leak in the yard line can all cause it. Sometimes the pump is fine and the well is simply producing less water than the house is asking for during peak use. We test each one.

    Submersible Well Pump Repair in Macon

    Submersible pumps hang inside the well casing on drop pipe, often 100 feet or more below the wellhead. The usual signs are no water at all, weak pressure or a hum with no flow. We test the motor from the surface first. When a pull is needed, a hoist brings the pipe up safely so we can replace the motor, the impellers or the check valve.

    Constant Pressure System Repair in Macon, GA

    Variable speed drives like the Grundfos SQE and Franklin SubDrive keep pressure steady by changing motor speed. When they fault, the drive shows a code that guides diagnosis. We troubleshoot the drive, the pressure sensor and the wiring, then repair or replace the failed part and verify the setpoint. These need a smaller tank.

    Franklin Electric Well Pump Service in Macon, GA

    Franklin Electric motors and control boxes are in a lot of local wells. We work on Franklin motors, QD control boxes and SubDrive units regularly. Common Franklin parts are on the truck. Replacing a failed motor usually costs less than a rewind once shipping is counted.

    Same-Day Well Pump Repair in Macon

    Well Pump Repair vs Replacement Cost in Macon, GA

A submersible pull and replacement runs about $1,800 to $5,000 and up, based on depth and horsepower. An older pump that already needs pulling is often worth replacing on the same trip. We lay out both options.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Well Pump Repair in Macon

    Minor repairs like a pressure switch or capacitor usually cost $150 to $500. A new pressure tank typically costs $600 to $1,500, while a deep submersible replacement runs from $1,800 past $5,000. We quote it before starting.

    Simple fixes like a pressure switch take a few hours at most. Replacing a submersible pump can take a full day, especially in a deep well.

    Start with the switch and the filter. Plenty of low-pressure calls come down to a dirty filter or a low switch setting. If those check out, a larger pressure tank, a constant pressure system or a booster pump may be the answer, and we can tell you which fits your well's yield.

    A leak is the first suspect. Other causes include a stuck switch, a waterlogged tank or an undersized pump. Our technicians in Macon, GA test each one.

    Goulds, Grundfos, Franklin Electric, Myers and Sta-Rite are all on the list, and HGR Plumbing works on most other brands too. Our technicians know the common models and system types.
