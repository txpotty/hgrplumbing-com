    What a Water Heater Service Visit Covers in Macon, GA

    Flushing a Tank Water Heater in Macon

    Popping and rumbling mean sediment. Calcium and magnesium settle on the bottom of the tank and form a layer that traps heat under it, so water boils in pockets beneath the scale and pops every time the burner or lower element fires. The tank drains until the water is clean. Heavy buildup may take several cycles.

    Tank Flush and Inspection in Macon

    Sediment forms from minerals in the water. We flush the tank, test the relief valve, check the anode and look over each fitting. Efficiency comes back. So does recovery time.

    Relief Valve and Safety Testing in Macon, GA

    The relief valve is the tank's last line of defense. We lift the lever, confirm water discharges and the valve reseats without dripping, and check that the discharge pipe runs full size to an approved spot within a few inches of the floor or outside. Leaky valves get replaced. Expansion tanks get an air charge check.

    Anode Rod Replacement in Macon

    Some water eats anode rods fast. Depending on your water chemistry, a rod that should last five years might be spent in three, so we pull it on every visit instead of assuming it is fine because the tank is still young. We swap worn ones. A healthy rod keeps the tank from rusting through.

    Water Heater Maintenance Plans in Macon, GA

    A plan takes the remembering off your plate. Members get a yearly or twice-yearly visit on the calendar, a reminder before it is due, priority scheduling if the heater acts up and discounted rates on any repair we find. Some homes need two visits. Service records stay with us.

    Gas Water Heater Safety Checks in Macon, GA

    Gas units need a clean burner and a good draft. Leak checks, flame inspection, burner cleaning and a draft test are part of every gas visit. CO is the risk. The thermocouple or flame sensor gets checked as well.

    Electric Element Service in Macon, GA

    Two elements, two thermostats. Sediment coats the lower element first, it runs hot and burns out, and that is the most common reason an electric tank suddenly gives you only half a tank of hot water. Power gets shut off first. Good parts stay in.
