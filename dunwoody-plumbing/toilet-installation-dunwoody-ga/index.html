    Kinds of Toilet Installation in Dunwoody, GA

    Smart Toilet and Bidet Seat Installation in Dunwoody

    Bidet seats like the TOTO Washlet and Bio Bidet bolt onto most existing elongated bowls. Water comes from a T-valve at the fill line. Powered seats with warm water and a dryer need a GFCI outlet within a few feet, and if one is not there, we line up a licensed electrician before we mount anything. Non-electric seats take about thirty minutes.

    Wall-Hung Toilet Installation in Dunwoody, GA

    Wall-hung toilets need a wall at least 2 by 6 deep, or a furred-out chase built in front of an existing 2 by 4 wall. Floors stay clear underneath. That makes mopping easier and lets you set the bowl height anywhere within the carrier's range, which is handy for tall households or anyone who wants a comfort-height seat without a tall bowl. Service happens through the flush plate.

    Smart Toilet Installation in Dunwoody, GA

    Smart toilets with heated seats and built-in bidets need two things the old toilet did not: power and a dedicated water connection. Most require a GFCI outlet within reach of the cord. We run the water side and mount the unit, and if a new outlet is needed we bring in a licensed electrician rather than splicing anything ourselves. Then we test every button.

    Comfort Height Toilet Installation in Dunwoody, GA

    Comfort height bowls stand about two inches taller than standard ones. Consider who uses the bathroom first. Shorter adults and kids sometimes find the taller seat leaves their feet dangling, while anyone with bad knees or a hip replacement usually finds it much easier to sit and stand. Installation is the same as a standard bowl.

    Comfort-Height and ADA Toilets in Dunwoody

    Aging in place usually starts in the bathroom. A taller toilet and a grab bar go a long way. If the drywall is already off, blocking gets added between studs so a grab bar can screw into wood, not hollow-wall anchors. We also center the bowl to ADA spacing.

    Wall-Hung Carrier Systems in Dunwoody

    In-wall carriers from Geberit and Grohe each mount a little differently, and the instructions matter. The frame height chart from the maker gets followed to the letter. A frame set wrong puts the bowl too low or too high, and once tile is up there is no adjusting it. Measure twice here.

    Pressure-Assisted Toilet Installation in Dunwoody

    Pressure-assisted toilets have a sealed vessel inside the tank that uses line pressure to push the flush. Below about 20 psi, they flush poorly. We check static pressure at a hose bib before recommending one, and we warn customers about the noise, since the flush is loud enough to hear across the house. They rarely clog.

    ADA-Compliant Toilet Installation in Dunwoody

    ADA toilets put the seat 17 to 19 inches off the floor, and the centerline sits 16 to 18 inches from the side wall. That spacing leaves room for a grab bar. We measure the existing drain before ordering, since moving a drain two inches to hit the ADA centerline means opening the floor. Elongated bowls are standard here.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Toilet Installation in Dunwoody

    Labor for a standard toilet installation in Dunwoody typically falls between $250 and $600, not counting the toilet itself. Flange repairs add to that. Extra work such as a rotted flange or soft subfloor raises the price, and we quote it before we begin.

    A one-piece toilet is cast as a single unit, so there is no tank-to-bowl joint to leak. It costs more and weighs more. A two-piece bolts the tank to the bowl with a rubber gasket, which makes it lighter to carry up stairs and cheaper to fix, since you can replace just the tank or just the bowl if one cracks. Both flush well.

    Rough-in is the distance from the finished wall to the center of the toilet drain. Most homes measure 12 inches. You will also see 10 and 14 inch rough-ins, and buying the wrong one means the toilet will not fit or sits well off the wall. Measure from the wall itself, not the baseboard.

    TOTO and American Standard do well in independent flush tests, and Kohler is close behind, with parts at most supply houses. For a budget, Gerber and Mansfield hold up. The right pick depends on your rough-in and who uses the room.

    Start with the rough-in, since that decides which models fit at all. Then pick round or elongated. An elongated bowl is roomier but adds about two inches of length, and that matters in a tight half bath. Check the MaP flush score too.

    Plan on one to three hours for a straight swap. Most of that is prep, not setting the bowl. Jobs that need a new flange or a patch of rotted subfloor can stretch toward half a day, and we will tell you on arrival if yours is one of them.

    Homeowners set basic toilets all the time. The trouble spots are flange height and bolt torque. A low flange needs an extender ring, and one extra turn on a nut can split the base, which is why new floors and wall-hung units should go to a pro.

    About two hours for most floor-mount toilets. Smart and wall-mounted units run longer. Wall-mounted units usually need two visits, carrier first and bowl after the finish wall, and a smart toilet can hinge on the electrician.
