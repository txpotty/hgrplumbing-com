    Frequently Asked Questions About Underground Pipe Repair in Brunswick

    The cost of underground pipe repair in Brunswick, GA can vary widely, typically ranging from $500 for minor spot repairs to $3,000 to $10,000 or more for trenchless full line replacements, or even more for extensive excavation and re-piping. Factors include the pipe material, depth, length of the damaged section, the repair method chosen and the extent of property restoration needed. We provide transparent, upfront pricing after a thorough diagnosis.

    The time of an underground pipe repair depends on the complexity. A minor leak repair might take a few hours, while a full trenchless sewer line replacement could take one to three days. We always aim for prompt service with minimal disruption.

    For water lines we usually use PEX or Type K copper, both approved for direct burial. For sewer lines we use Schedule 40 PVC. The right choice depends on the existing pipe and local code.

    Trenchless pipe repair offers several key advantages, including minimal disruption to your yard, landscaping and concrete, which can save on restoration costs. It's often faster than traditional excavation and less invasive, preserving the look and value of your property. This method is also durable, often extending the lifespan of your pipes.

    In most cities, the homeowner owns and maintains the water line from the meter to the house. The utility is responsible for the meter and the line on the street side. We can help confirm which side the leak is on.

    The length of an underground pipe repair depends heavily on the severity and location of the damage, as well as the repair method chosen. Minor repairs using trenchless techniques might be completed in a single day. More extensive issues, such as full sewer line replacements requiring excavation, could take two to five days, including restoration time.

    Common signs include a water bill that jumps without explanation, a meter that moves with all water off, low pressure and wet or sinking spots in the yard. A hissing sound near the meter box can also signal a leak.

    Shallow irrigation leaks are sometimes a manageable DIY repair. Main water lines and sewer laterals require locating, utility marking and code-compliant fittings, so a licensed plumber is the safer choice.
